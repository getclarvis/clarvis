# Code of conduct

Clarvis aims to be a technically rigorous and welcoming project. Participation in project spaces
requires respect for other people, their time, identities, experience levels, and boundaries.

## Expected behavior

- Discuss ideas and evidence without attacking people.
- Give actionable feedback and accept good-faith correction.
- Keep review focused on the contribution and the project's documented constraints.
- Make room for newcomers, different communication styles, and accessibility needs.
- Protect private information and stop contact when someone asks you to stop.

Harassment, discrimination, threats, sexualized attention, deliberate humiliation, doxxing,
malicious disruption, and retaliation are not acceptable. This applies in repositories, issues,
pull requests, reviews, project chat, events, and private communication undertaken as a project
representative.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove content, close or lock threads, reject contributions, issue warnings,
or temporarily or permanently restrict participation. Decisions should consider context, impact,
pattern, and the safety of affected people rather than public argument about a reporter.

Use GitHub's blocking and abuse-reporting tools for urgent platform safety concerns. Do not put
sensitive conduct details in a public issue. Before the public launch, the project must publish a
dedicated confidential conduct contact in the organization profile and this document; that owner
action is tracked in the [OSS launch checklist](docs/oss-launch-checklist.md).

Good-faith reports and appeals will be handled as privately as the available channel permits. A
security advisory is reserved for software vulnerabilities and must not be used as a general conduct
mailbox.
